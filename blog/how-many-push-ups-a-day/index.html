How Many Push-Ups Should You Do a Day?

How many push-ups a day to do, based on one honest test set: a table by level, easier versions, rest days, and what the 40 push-up study really found.

Crhistian C·Nov 2, 2026·8 min read

TL;DR

  • There's no proven universal number. Test one clean set and use about half of it per set, two to four sets a day.
  • Can't do one from the floor? Start with hands on a counter or wall; lower the surface as you get stronger.
  • Daily push-ups are fine at moderate effort; take one or two days off a week and skip max sets most days.
  • Guidelines call for strength work on 2+ days a week for all major muscles, so add squats and a pulling move.
  • The "40 push-ups" heart study was observational and only in male firefighters: a fitness signal, not a daily target.

You saw someone online doing 100 push-ups a day for 30 days. Day one, you got to 22, then did the last eight on your knees, sort of.

Day two, your chest hurt when you reached for your phone. Day three, you didn't do any.

The number wasn't the problem. It was someone else's number.

So here's the honest answer to how many push-ups a day you should do: no study has found one daily number that's right for everyone. The useful number is about half of what you can do in one good set today, done in a few sets, on most days but not every day at full effort. Below is how to find yours, what to do if it's zero, and what the research does and doesn't say.

How many push-ups a day: start with one test set

Before you pick a number, find out where you are. It takes two minutes.

  1. Warm up. Ten arm circles each way, then five easy push-ups against a counter or wall.
  2. Do one set of floor push-ups with good form. Hands a bit wider than your shoulders, body in a straight line from head to heels, chest down until your elbows are at about 90 degrees, then all the way back up.
  3. Stop when your form breaks, not when you collapse. If your hips sag, your butt pokes up or you start doing half reps, that set is over.
  4. Write down the number. That's your max. It's your starting point, not a grade.

If you couldn't do one from the floor, that's common and fine. Do the same test with your hands on a kitchen counter, and use that number instead.

Your daily push-up number, by level

The rule of thumb: sets of about half your max, two to four sets, with a minute or two of rest between them. Each set should end with a few reps still in the tank. This isn't a formula from a study. It's a conservative way to practice a lot without grinding yourself down, which matters more when you plan to do it often.

That works out to somewhere between 15 and 60 push-ups a day for most people. Less than the internet challenge. Far more than the zero you'd do after quitting it on day three.

Retest every two weeks. When your max goes up, your sets go up.

About knee push-ups

Knee push-ups work, but many people find incline push-ups (hands on a counter, bed or sturdy table) carry over better to the floor version, because you still hold a straight line from head to heels. You can make them harder in small steps by using a lower surface. Pick whichever feels better on your wrists and shoulders.

Is it bad to do push-ups every day?

Not for most healthy people, as long as you're not going all-out every time. Daily push-ups at the moderate amounts above are a lot like daily walking: practice, not punishment.

Where people get into trouble is doing a max set, or a 100-rep challenge, every single day. Muscles adapt between sessions, and sore, tired muscles tend to mean worse form, which is where shoulder and elbow aches come from.

A simple weekly shape that works:

  • 4–5 days: your daily plan from the table, every set leaving a few reps in reserve.
  • 1 day: a harder session, one extra set or a slightly harder variation.
  • 1–2 days: off from push-ups. Walk, stretch, do squats.

Normal muscle soreness the next day is fine; do an easier version or skip a day. Sharp pain, pain in a joint, or pain that's still there after a few days is not normal. Stop and get it checked.

Push-ups only cover half the job

The US Physical Activity Guidelines recommend muscle-strengthening activity on at least two days a week that works all the major muscle groups: legs, hips, back, abdomen, chest, shoulders and arms. Push-ups are named as an example, and they're a good one for the chest, shoulders, arms and core.

They don't do much for your legs or back. So if push-ups are your daily habit, pair them with something like bodyweight squats, and something that pulls, like a row or a pull-up when you're ready. Ten squats after each push-up set is an easy fix.

And yes, push-ups can build real strength. In a small 2017 trial, 18 men were randomly assigned to bench press or push-ups at a matched load, twice a week for eight weeks; both groups gained chest muscle thickness and bench press strength by similar amounts. A 2015 trial with 30 students found band-loaded push-ups and bench press produced similar strength gains, though the bench press group gained a little more. Both studies were small and short, but they point the same way: you don't need a gym for this.

How many push-ups should you be able to do?

This is where the number "40" comes from.

In a 2019 study from Harvard's T.H. Chan School of Public Health, published in JAMA Network Open, researchers looked at 1,104 male firefighters (average age about 40) who did a push-up test paced by a metronome. Over roughly ten years, 37 had a cardiovascular event. Men who could do more than 40 push-ups at the start had a 96% lower rate of those events than men who could do fewer than 10. Push-up capacity was more strongly linked to risk than a submaximal treadmill test was.

40+ push-upsLinked to a 96% lower rate of cardiovascular events vs. under 10, in an observational study of 1,104 male firefighters (Yang et al., 2019).

Read that carefully, though. It was observational: it shows a link, not that doing push-ups protects your heart. The men who could do 40 were probably fitter in lots of other ways. Everyone was a male firefighter, so the authors said the results may not apply to women, other ages or less active people. And it was about a single test, not a daily target.

The useful takeaway is narrower and still good: push-up capacity is a quick, free signal of overall fitness, and it's one you can watch improve. Your test set every two weeks is that signal.

Questions people ask

Is 100 push-ups a day too many?

For someone who can already do 40 or more in a set, 100 spread across the day in sets of 15–25 can be reasonable. For a beginner, it usually means bad reps and sore joints by day three. Start with the table and earn your way up.

Should I do them all at once or spread out?

Either works. Splitting them across the day, a set in the morning, one at lunch, one in the evening, is easier to recover from and easier to fit in. It's the same idea as exercise snacks.

When should I check with a doctor first?

If you have a heart condition, high blood pressure, a shoulder, elbow or wrist injury, are pregnant, or haven't exercised in a long time, check with a doctor before starting, and begin with wall or counter push-ups.
