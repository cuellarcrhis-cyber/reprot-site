Rep Rot

Privacy Policy

Effective October 30, 2026

The short version
  • No accounts, no ads, no tracking, and no third-party analytics.
  • Your workouts, settings and name stay on your iPhone.
  • The camera counts reps on your phone. Video is never recorded or uploaded.
  • Rep Rot can't see which apps you block or how long you use them.
  • By default, the only thing that leaves your phone is a small "finished a set today" record (a date and an exercise name, nothing personal), used to show how many people did the same workout.
  • If you turn on "Help improve rep counting", your answers to "How did the count go?" are also sent anonymously, plus movement points (a stick figure, never video or photos) for sets that were miscounted. It's off unless you turn it on.

What stays on your iPhone

Rep Rot stores the following only on your device. We never receive it, and it's deleted when you delete the app.

  • The first name you enter (optional), used only for the greeting on the home screen.
  • Your workout history: the dates you completed sets, the exercise, and the number of reps or seconds.
  • Your settings: exercise, daily goal, alarms and app usage limits.
  • "Rots prevented": the times an app limit blocked an app, used for your counts and calendar.
  • Rep-count feedback: when you answer "How did the count go?" after a set, your rating, and for sets that were miscounted or looked off, a log of that set's body movement points (see below). The last 20 are kept.

Camera

When you do a workout, Rep Rot uses the front camera and Apple's on-device Vision framework to detect your body position and count reps. Camera frames are analyzed in real time on your iPhone and immediately discarded. Nothing is recorded, saved or sent anywhere.

Screen Time (app limits)

App limits use Apple's Screen Time frameworks (Family Controls, Device Activity and Managed Settings), with permission you grant for your own device. When you choose apps, iOS gives Rep Rot opaque tokens, not app names, and iOS itself tracks your usage and applies the block. Rep Rot can't see which apps you picked, your browsing, or how long you used anything, and none of this data leaves your device. You can remove Rep Rot's Screen Time access at any time in Settings → Screen Time, which also removes any blocks.

Alarms and notifications

Alarms (Apple's AlarmKit) and notifications are scheduled locally on your iPhone. No notification content is sent to us.

The "people did this today" count

When you finish a set, and if you're signed in to iCloud, Rep Rot saves a small record to Apple's CloudKit public database. It contains only:

  • the date (for example, 2026-10-18), and
  • the exercise name (for example, Push-Ups).

The record has a random name. CloudKit also keeps a pseudonymous identifier for whoever created it, which is specific to Rep Rot and is how each person counts only once. It is not your name, email or Apple ID, and we can't use it to identify you. These records are used only to show how many people completed the same exercise that day, and we classify them as fitness data used for app functionality, not linked to you and not used for tracking.

Rep Rot deletes your records from previous days the next time you open the app. If you delete the app or stop using it, your last record can stay in the database; email us and we'll remove old records. If you're not signed in to iCloud, nothing is saved. Apple processes this data under the Apple Privacy Policy.

"Help improve rep counting" (optional)

Sometimes after a set, Rep Rot asks how accurate the count was. You can skip it, or turn the question off in Triggers → Rep counting. Your answer is saved on your iPhone.

If you turn on Help improve rep counting (it's off by default), your answers are sent to Apple's CloudKit public database so we can find and fix counting mistakes. Each one contains:

  • your star rating, the count Rep Rot showed and the count you entered;
  • the exercise, your goal, your iPhone model, iOS version and Rep Rot version;
  • for sets that were miscounted or looked off, also: how long the set took, when each rep counted, and body movement points (the positions of joints like shoulders, elbows and knees as numbers, about 15 times a second: a stick figure, not an image).

It never includes video, photos, your name, location, or which apps you use. The record has a random name and isn't linked to you; only the Rep Rot team can read it, and we use it only to improve rep counting. We classify it as diagnostic data used for app functionality, not linked to you and not used for tracking. Turn the switch off anytime to stop sending; email us to delete what was sent.

If you use Report a problem, the email Rep Rot drafts includes your app version, iOS version and iPhone model, plus the last set you said was miscounted (movement points only). You can read and remove it before sending.

What we don't do

  • We don't sell, rent or share your data.
  • We don't use advertising, tracking or third-party analytics SDKs.
  • We don't track you across other apps or websites.

Your choices and deleting data

  • Delete everything on your phone: delete the Rep Rot app. Your most recent "finished a set" record may stay in the count database; email us and we'll remove it.
  • Stop the anonymous daily count: turn off iCloud for Rep Rot in Settings → [your name] → iCloud.
  • Rep-count feedback: turn off "Ask how the count went" or "Help improve rep counting" in Rep Rot → Triggers → Rep counting.
  • Remove app limits: turn them off in Rep Rot, or remove Screen Time access in Settings → Screen Time.
  • Questions or deletion requests: email help@reprotapp.com and we'll reply within 30 days.

Children

Rep Rot is not directed to children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from them.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update this page and the date above. Significant changes will also be noted in the app's release notes.
