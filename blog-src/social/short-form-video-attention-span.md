2. **The big review:** A 2025 meta-analysis pooled 71 studies and about 98,000 people. Heavier short-video use went with weaker attention and impulse control. It's a link, not proof of cause.

**Thread:**
1. "Short videos destroyed my attention span." Here's what the research actually shows, minus the brain-rot headlines.
2. The biggest evidence: a 2025 Psychological Bulletin meta-analysis, 71 studies, ~98,000 people. More short-form use, weaker attention and impulse control. Correlational, mostly one-time snapshots.
3. The useful finding: plain hours were only weakly linked. Scales measuring compulsive use (can't stop, lose track of time) were linked much more strongly.
4. Lab experiments show a short hangover: a few minutes of TikTok before a task made people skim more or forget planned actions. Small studies, one sitting.
5. And it comes back. In a trial of 467 adults, two weeks of blocked mobile internet improved sustained attention.
6. So don't aim for zero. Aim at the moment you can't stop: an end point on the feed, no scrolling before focus work, and something physical to do with the urge. https://reprotapp.com/blog/short-form-video-attention-span/
