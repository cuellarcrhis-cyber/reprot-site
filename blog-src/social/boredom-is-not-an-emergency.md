## 1. Instagram carousel (7 slides)

1. **Hook:** The kettle needs 90 seconds. Your hand is already in your pocket.
2. **What boredom is:** Psychologists define it as wanting to be engaged and not being able to. It's a want, not a malfunction.
3. **Does the phone fix it?** In a study of 83 PhD students, boredom made a phone pickup about 1.4× as likely, and boredom was slightly higher afterward, not lower. (Small, observational study.)
4. **The real driver:** A 2026 study of 700+ people: the more you dislike boredom, the more you scroll. The alarm matters more than the feeling.
5. **Some boredom helps:** In small lab studies, people who did a dull task first came up with more creative ideas.
6. **Save this, the 2-minute rule:** Catch the reach. Name it: "bored." Wait two minutes. Ask what it wants: a gap, a break, movement or people.
7. **CTA:** If it's movement, keep it tiny: 10 squats or 10 counter push-ups. Full post at the link in bio.

## 4. Newsletter blurb

**Subject:** Boredom isn't an emergency
**Preview:** Why the quick phone check doesn't actually help. And what to do instead.

You grab your phone in the 90 seconds a kettle takes, and you don't remember deciding to. This week we looked at the research on boredom and phone use. One study that logged real phone use found boredom made pickups more likely and left people slightly more bored afterward. A 2026 study found the bigger driver is how much we dislike being bored. So the fix isn't removing boredom. It's lowering the alarm: a two-minute rule, and a little movement.
