## 1. Instagram carousel

1. **Hook:** 100 reps. No gym. Does it actually build muscle?
2. **The method:** One exercise. 100 good reps. As few sets as it takes. Rest as long as you need. Next time: fewer sets.
3. **Why it can work:** In a small 8-week trial, sets of 25–35 light reps grew muscle about as well as 8–12 heavy reps. The catch: every set went to failure.
4. **The real rule:** It's not the number 100. It's that the last reps of each set are hard. Easy reps are practice, not a growth signal.
5. **Pick the right version:** You should manage 10–25 in a row. Can do 60? Make the move harder (feet on a chair), not the number bigger.
6. **Beginner version:** 50 reps. Counter or knee push-ups, chair squats. Stop a rep or two before failure. Twice a week.
7. **Save this:** Warm up 3 min → sets to near-failure → rest 60–90 s → stop at 100 → write down your set count → beat it next time. Sharp or joint pain = stop.
8. **CTA:** The hard part is set one. Rep Rot locks the apps you lose hours to until you get up and move. Full guide at the link in bio.

**Thread:**
1. Thread: "Can bodyweight build muscle?" Yes, with one condition most people skip.
2. A small 2015 trial: 18 trained men, 8 weeks. Light sets of 25–35 reps vs heavy sets of 8–12, all to failure. Similar muscle growth. Heavy won on max strength.
3. A 2017 meta-analysis of 21 studies found the same pattern: growth similar across loads when sets go to failure; heavy loads build more max strength.
4. The condition: sets end close to failure. Ten easy push-ups when you could do thirty is practice, not a growth signal.
5. So the 100-rep method works when you pick a variation you can only do 10–25 times. Beginners: 50 reps, easier versions, twice a week.
