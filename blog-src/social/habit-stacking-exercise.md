## 1. Instagram carousel (7 slides)

1. **Hook:** "After coffee, I'll do a 20-minute workout." Day 4: cold coffee, phone in hand. The stack didn't fail. The workout was too heavy.
2. **Habit stacking:** "After I [something you already do], I will [new habit]." The old habit does the remembering.
3. **The 10-rep rule:** the set must be doable on your worst morning, in what you slept in, in under a minute. For most people: about ten reps.
4. **Why small:** a study of 111 new gym members found frequency (about 4x a week for 6 weeks) was what built the habit. Small sets are easy to do often.
5. **Honest check:** habit stacking itself is barely studied. If-then plans and repeating in the same moment are. That's what you're borrowing.
6. **Save this:** Coffee maker on → 10 counter push-ups. Teeth brushed → 10 squats. Phone on charger → 20-second plank. Too hard? Wall push-ups, sit-to-stand from the bed, knees-down plank.
7. **CTA:** The one anchor you never forget is your phone. Full post, stack table and safety notes: link in bio.

## 3. X / Threads

**Thread:**
1. Habit stacking: "After I [current habit], I will [new habit]." Popularized by James Clear, built on BJ Fogg's Tiny Habits recipe.
2. Honest bit: habit stacking itself has barely been tested. One small flossing study (50 people), and a larger follow-up didn't repeat the result.
3. What is tested: if-then plans. A 2013 meta-analysis of 26 studies found a small-to-medium boost to physical activity. Real, not magic.
4. Habits come from frequency. A study of 111 new gym members found about four sessions a week for six weeks was the minimum to form an exercise habit.
5. So keep the set tiny: about ten reps. Coffee maker → counter push-ups. Teeth brushed → squats. Phone on charger → 20-second plank.
6. Ten is the floor, not a full plan. Grow it once it's automatic. Table, form cues and FAQ: reprotapp.com/blog/habit-stacking-exercise/
