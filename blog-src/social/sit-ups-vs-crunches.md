## 1. Instagram carousel (7 slides)

1. **Hook:** Your sit-ups burn in your thighs, not your stomach. Here's why.
2. **The difference:** Crunch: shoulder blades up, lower back down. Sit-up: all the way up. The second half is mostly your hip flexors.
3. **Start here:** Crunches are shorter, easier to control and heavy on the abs. Good first pick.
4. **The back question:** Lab work on pig spines flags repeated rounding. Big trials in thousands of soldiers found no difference in injuries or back pain.
5. **Back pain now?** Skip sit-ups. Use dead bugs and planks, and check with a doctor or physical therapist.
6. **Save this ladder:** Dead bug → partial crunch → crunch → sit-up, feet free. Move up at 2 × 12 steady reps.
7. **CTA:** A few sets of 8–15 good reps, 2–3 days a week. Full guide and a 2-week plan: link in bio.

**Thread:**
1. Sit-ups or crunches? The difference is how far you come up.
2. Crunch: head and shoulder blades off the floor, lower back stays down. Sit-up: all the way up, and the second half is mostly your hip flexors.
3. Are sit-ups bad for your back? Lab studies on pig spines say repeated rounding under load can injure discs. That's a machine, for hours.
4. Trials in thousands of U.S. soldiers found no difference in injuries or back pain between programs with sit-ups and core stability work. Young, fit recruits, so not the last word either.
5. So: healthy back, sit-ups in sensible amounts are fine. Back pain now, skip them, use dead bugs and planks, and see a doctor.
