## 1. Instagram carousel

1. **Hook:** No time to work out? You have 5 minutes. Slow them down.
2. **The trick:** Every rep has a lowering half. Take 3–5 seconds on the way down, come up at normal speed. Same moves, more work.
3. **The study:** 22 inactive adults (32–69) did about 5 minutes a day of slow-lowering moves for 4 weeks. On average: strength up ~13%, push-ups to failure up ~66%.
4. **The honest part:** Small study, no separate control group, results varied a lot. It shows 5 minutes beats nothing. It doesn't replace a full program.
5. **It doesn't have to hurt:** A 2026 review from the same researcher argues exhaustion and soreness aren't required to get stronger.
6. **Save this, the routine:** 10 slow reps each, daily. Chair squat. Wall push-up. Chair recline. Heel drop. 3 s down to start, build to 5.
7. **Progress:** When 10 feel easy, make the move harder (lower seat, counter push-ups). Don't make the lowering longer than ~5 s.
8. **CTA:** You don't lack five minutes. You lose them to a feed. Rep Rot locks the apps you lose hours to until you get up and move. Link in bio.

## 3. X / Threads

**Single post:** The workout you skip because it's "only 5 minutes" might be the one that works. Slow the lowering of each rep to 3–5 seconds. In a small 2025 study, inactive adults doing that for ~5 min a day got measurably stronger in 4 weeks. https://reprotapp.com/blog/slow-eccentric-reps/

**Thread:**
1. You don't need a 45-minute workout to make progress. You need to slow down the part of the rep you're rushing.
2. Every rep has a lowering half (the eccentric phase). Muscles produce more force there, at a lower energy cost. Take 3–5 seconds down, come up normally.
3. A 2025 Edith Cowan University study: 22 sedentary adults, 32–69, ~5 min a day for 4 weeks. Chair squats, wall push-ups, chair reclines, heel drops. Strength up ~13% on average; push-ups to failure up ~66%.
4. Caveats: small, no separate control group, big spread between people, no change in body composition or blood pressure. It shows 5 minutes beats nothing, not that it beats a full program.
5. Don't overdo the slowness: a meta-analysis found reps from 0.5 to ~8 s grew muscle similarly; very slow reps (10 s+) looked worse.
6. Full routine with easier and harder versions, plus safety notes: https://reprotapp.com/blog/slow-eccentric-reps/
