Title: Push-Ups and Wrist Pain: 7 Fixes, Easiest First

## 1. Instagram carousel (7 slides)

1. **Hook:** Push-ups hurt your wrists? Your arms aren't the problem.
2. **Why:** A floor push-up puts about 64% of your body weight through your hands, on a wrist bent back about as far as it goes.
3. **Fix 1–2:** Hands under shoulders. Fingers spread. Press through the whole hand. Two minutes of wrist warm-up first.
4. **Fix 3:** Raise your hands. A counter or table takes weight off your wrists, and it's still a real push-up.
5. **Fix 4–6:** Straighten the wrist: a towel under the heel of your hand, fists, fixed handles or hex dumbbells.
6. **Fix 7:** Everything still hurts? Forearm plank for a week or two, then build back slowly.
7. **Save this:** Stop and see a doctor for tingling, numbness, severe pain, or pain that hasn't eased after two weeks. Full guide at the link in bio.

**Caption:**
Wrist pain is one of the most common reasons people give up on push-ups. Usually it's fixable: change the angle, the load or the grip. A small 2018 lab study (14 people) found forces travel differently through a bent-back wrist, and the researchers suggested a straight wrist is likely safer. Seven fixes, easiest first, plus a 4-week way back to flat hands. If you have a wrist injury, arthritis, tingling or numbness, check with a doctor first. **Thread:**
1. Push-ups hurting your wrists usually isn't weak wrists. It's the position.
2. A floor push-up puts about 64% of your body weight through your hands (force-plate study, 23 adults), on a wrist bent back about as far as it goes.
3. A 2018 lab study of 14 people found forces move differently through a bent-back wrist. The authors suggest a straight wrist is likely safer. Small study, but a clear direction.
4. So: set your hands well, warm up, raise your hands onto a counter, or keep the wrist straight with a towel, fists, fixed handles or hex dumbbells.
5. Still hurts? Forearm plank for a week or two. Tingling, numbness, severe pain or two weeks without improvement: see a doctor.
