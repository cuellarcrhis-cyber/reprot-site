## 1. Instagram carousel (7 slides)

1. **Hook:** "It takes 23 minutes to refocus after a notification." Not quite.
2. The number comes from Gloria Mark's research at UC Irvine. It's the average time before people *went back* to an interrupted task, not how long their brain took to reboot.
3. In between, people usually worked on about two other things first. And the spread was huge: minutes for some, hours for others.
4. The part that is solid: in a lab study, even a notification people ignored led to more mistakes on an attention task. (Stothart et al., 2015)
5. And about half of interruptions are self-interruptions, Mark says. No buzz. Your hand just goes for the phone.
6. **Save this:** Before you switch, write one line: where you stopped, what's next. Batch notifications (Settings → Notifications → Scheduled Summary). Use a Focus for deep work. Phone in another room.
7. **CTA:** Full post, with what's known and what isn't, at the link in bio.

**Thread:**
1. You've seen "it takes 23 minutes to refocus after an interruption." Here's what that number actually is, and what to do instead.
2. It comes from Gloria Mark's work at UC Irvine: the average time before interrupted work was picked back up. People usually did about two other things in between, and the spread ran from minutes to hours.
3. What is well supported: in a 2015 lab study, people who got a notification and didn't even answer it made more errors on an attention task.
4. Leftover thoughts about the unfinished task follow you to the next one. Researchers call it attention residue. A quick "ready-to-resume" note before switching reduced it (Leroy & Glomb, 2018).
5. On iPhone: Settings → Notifications → Scheduled Summary to batch the rest into a few deliveries a day. In a 2-week trial, three batches a day beat both "as usual" and "none at all."
