## 1. Instagram carousel (7 slides)

1. **Hook:** Saturday: no phone, no music, no fun. Sunday night: five hours of videos. The dopamine detox didn't fail you. The plan did.
2. **The myth:** You can't flush dopamine. Your brain makes it all day; you need it to move, learn and want things.
3. **The origin:** The psychologist who popularized "dopamine fasting" says the name isn't meant literally. The method is stimulus control: change the cues.
4. **The evidence:** Trials that limited social media for 2–4 weeks found small, real benefits. Not from a heroic weekend. From less of one thing, for weeks.
5. **Friction instead:** Feed notifications off. App off the Home Screen. Grayscale. Charger out of the bedroom. Pick two.
6. **Save this:** When the urge hits: 10 squats (easier: sit-to-stand from a chair), 10 counter push-ups, or a 20-second plank on your knees.
7. **CTA:** Fasts end. Speed bumps don't. Full post with the studies and exact iPhone settings: link in bio.

**Thread:**
1. The weekend dopamine detox: no phone, no music, no fun. Then Sunday night you watch five hours of videos. Here's why, and what works instead.
2. Dopamine isn't a toxin that builds up from fun. It's a messenger your brain uses to move, learn and want. You can't drain it by staring at a wall.
3. The psychologist who popularized "dopamine fasting" has said the title isn't meant literally. The method underneath is stimulus control from CBT: change the cues around a habit.
4. Trials that limited or paused social media for 2–4 weeks found small, real gains. In one, people kept using the app less after it ended. Defaults changed, not brain chemistry.
5. So skip the fast. Pick your one or two worst habits and add speed bumps: feed notifications off, app off the Home Screen, grayscale, charger out of the bedroom.
