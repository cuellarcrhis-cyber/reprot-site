## Search intent

Someone who keeps not starting wants a concrete way to fit exercise into the
day they already have: which existing habit to attach it to, and how much to do.

## Must-cover

- What habit stacking is and the formula ("After I…, I will…"); Clear / Fogg origin
- Concrete exercise examples tied to daily anchors
- Start tiny, then build
- Why it works (cues, less reliance on willpower)
- How long until it's automatic

## Questions people ask

- Does habit stacking work? (Barely studied directly; its building blocks are: if-then plans, context-dependent repetition. One small flossing study, not replicated in a larger one.)
- How long does it take for a stacked habit to stick? (Lally 2010: median 66 days, 18–254 range; a single miss didn't derail it.)
- Is 10 reps a day enough? (It's the floor that builds the habit, not a full program; guidelines: muscle-strengthening 2+ days a week; tiny daily efforts can still produce measurable change in small trials.)

## Our edge

1. Honest evidence section: habit stacking itself has almost no direct trials (Milkman, via APS); the parts it's made of do, with real effect sizes and caveats. Nobody in the top 5 says this.
2. The 10-rep rule as a clear point of view: the set must survive your worst day. Ties to Kaushal & Rhodes (frequency builds habits).
3. A table of precise anchors → set → easier version, plus form cues and safety.
4. A "why stacks fail" fix list (vague anchor, weekday-only anchor, wrong place, set too big).

- Judah, Gardner & Aunger 2013, Br J Health Psychol 18(2):338–353 (N = 50, floss after vs before brushing; after → stronger habit; prospective memory predicted flossing; exploratory). PMID 22989272. Judah 2015 PhD thesis (n = 118) did not find a placement effect.
- Lally et al. 2010, EJSP (96 people, median 66 days, 18–254; one missed day didn't matter), via BPS Research Digest
- Kaushal & Rhodes 2015, J Behav Med 38(4):652–663 (111 new gym members, 12 weeks; ≥4 sessions/week for ~6 weeks the minimum to establish an exercise habit). PMID 25851609
- Sato et al. 2022, Scand J Med Sci Sports 32(5):833–843 (3-s max contraction, once a day, 5 days/week, 4 weeks; 13 per group; eccentric group gained strength in all measures; no change in muscle thickness). PMID 35104387
