## Search intent

Someone who can't do one floor push-up wants a clear, doable plan that gets them there, and to know where to start today.

## Must-cover

- Where to start (wall / incline) and a way to test your level
- Form cues and common mistakes (sagging hips, flared elbows, half reps)
- Incline vs knee push-ups
- Negatives (slow lowering) and plank holds
- How often to train and when to move to the next step
- How long it takes, honestly

## Our edge

1. A "ladder" of household surfaces (wall, counter, table, sofa seat, stair) ordered by load, backed by Ebben 2011 force-plate data (hands on a ~61 cm box ≈41% of body mass vs ≈64% for a floor push-up; knee ≈49%).
2. A 4-week plan with a progress rule (move down a rung when all sets are clean), not a fixed calendar, and an honest "repeat a week, that's normal".
3. Suprak 2011: the bottom of the push-up is the hardest part (≈75% vs ≈69% of body mass in the up position), which explains why people fail at the bottom and why negatives help.
4. Safety notes and scale-downs at every step; US guidelines for 2+ strength days per week.
