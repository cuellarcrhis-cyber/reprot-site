## Search intent

"My wrists hurt when I do push-ups. Tell me what to change today so I can keep doing them, and whether I should worry."

## Must-cover

- Why: wrist bent back under load
- Form: hands under shoulders, fingers spread, press through the whole hand
- Warm-up / mobility
- Fist (knuckle) push-ups; handles or dumbbells; towel or wedge under the heel of the hand
- Incline or knee push-ups to reduce load
- Forearm plank as a no-wrist alternative
- When to stop and see a doctor

## Our edge

1. The one real study on this (Polovinets 2018, 14 volunteers, force plates + motion capture): forces travel differently through a bent-back wrist; authors conclude a neutral wrist is likely safer. Told honestly (small, lab, no injury outcomes).
2. A fixes table ordered easiest first with "what it changes" and "best if", instead of an unordered listicle.
3. Load numbers so the incline fix makes sense (≈64% of body mass on the hands on the floor vs ≈41% hands raised ~61 cm; Ebben 2011).
4. Clear, early stop signs from NHS (tingling, numbness, can't hold things, not better after 2 weeks), without a medication recommendation.

## Facts used

- Polovinets O, Wolf A, Wollstein R. J Hand Ther 2018;31(3):322–330, PMID 28684196: 14 volunteers, two force plates + motion capture; forces travelled more dorsally and ulnarly over a wider area in hyperextension; authors: neutral-wrist push-ups "likely safer because ligaments may be preferentially loaded in hyperextension"; call for further study. Abstract read via Europe PMC.
- Ebben et al. 2011 (23 adults): floor push-up ≈64% body mass; hands on ~61 cm box ≈41%; knees ≈49%. Suprak 2011: ≈75% at the bottom. (Verified for how-to-do-your-first-push-up.)
- ACE-sponsored study 2009 (UW–La Crosse, 13 trained male students, EMG): rotating push-up handles raised chest, front-shoulder and triceps activation in standard and wide positions, likely due to 4–5 inches more range of motion; ACE physiologist found pivoting handles made a neutral wrist harder to keep. Read from the ACE PDF.
- NHS wrist pain page: see a GP if pain stops normal activities, not better after 2 weeks of home care, or tingling/loss of sensation; contact 111 for severe pain, can't move wrist or hold things, or a snap/grinding/popping at injury.
