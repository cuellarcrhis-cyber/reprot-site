## Search intent

Someone who feels overstimulated wants to know whether a dopamine detox works,
and if not, what to do instead this week.

## Must-cover

- What a dopamine detox is and where it came from (Sepah, 2019, "dopamine fasting 2.0")
- Why the name is wrong (you can't lower or flush dopamine)
- Whether it works (no direct research; related evidence on breaks and limits)
- How to do a sane version (choose a few behaviors, set times, replace)
- Risks of extreme versions (rebound, isolation)

## Questions people ask

- Does dopamine detox actually work? (Not as a chemical reset; breaks and limits on specific behaviors have some trial evidence.)
- How long should a dopamine detox be? (Sepah: scheduled "fast" windows; Lembke suggests about a month for a compulsive behavior; the evidence studies ran 1–4 weeks.)
- Is a dopamine detox bad for you? (Extreme versions cutting out music, talking, exercise are criticized as unhealthy; if you're struggling with mood, see a professional.)

## Our edge

1. A clear point of view: the useful part of "dopamine fasting" was always stimulus control, i.e. friction. Name it and do that.
2. Honest evidence: three trials (Hunt 2018 limits, Allcott 2020 deactivation, Castelo 2025 mobile-internet block) plus grayscale studies, each with sample and caveat. Breaks help somewhat; the effects that last come from changed defaults.
3. A friction ladder table with exact iOS 27 paths (Color Filters grayscale checked on Apple's page).
4. Exercise as the replacement with an easier version and safety note; mental-health note pointing to professional help and 988.
