## Search intent

A routine they can follow right now, without equipment, that fits in five
minutes and doesn't need a gym.

## Must-cover

- The routine itself, timed (one move per minute is the common shape)
- No equipment; bedroom-sized space
- Squats, push-up variant, glute bridge, plank, a light warm-up
- Beginner modifications for each move
- Whether 5 minutes is "enough"

## Questions people ask

- Is a 5-minute workout enough? (Any amount counts per the 2018 guidelines;
  5 min × 7 days = 35 min, so it's a start, not the 150-minute weekly target.)
- Is it OK to exercise right after waking? (Yes for most people; sleep
  inertia ~20 min, morning lightheadedness: sit first, stand slowly.)
- Will it wake me up? (Small trial: 10 min easy stair walking felt more
  energizing than 50 mg caffeine; short-lived, 18 young women.)

## Our edge

1. Built for a just-woken body: the bed is the equipment (sit-to-stand,
   incline push-up on a sturdy frame) and the first 30 seconds are sitting.
2. Honest benefit claims: the stair-vs-caffeine trial with its caveats; no
   fat-burn or metabolism promises.
3. A table with easier and harder versions for every minute, plus a 4-week
   ladder.
4. The night-before setup that makes it happen (phone across the room, a
   cleared patch of floor), linked to our get-out-of-bed post.

## Sources

- Randolph DD, O'Connor PJ 2017, "Stair walking is more energizing than low
  dose caffeine in sleep deprived young women," Physiology & Behavior,
  doi:10.1016/j.physbeh.2017.03.013 (18 women, <6.5 h sleep, 10 min
  low-to-moderate stair walking vs 50 mg caffeine vs placebo; bigger rise in
  feelings of energy, short-lived; no change in attention or memory)
- CDC, Adult Activity: An Overview (150 min moderate/wk, 2+ days
  muscle-strengthening; any amount gives some benefit)
- health.gov, Top 10 Things to Know About the 2nd edition of the Physical
  Activity Guidelines (10-minute bout rule dropped)
- Sleep Research Society fact sheet on sleep inertia (Hilditch & Vincent)
  (grogginess usually fades within ~20 min)
- Harvard Health, "Dizzy spells when you stand up: when should you worry?"
  (clench leg muscles before standing; tell a doctor about fainting or falls)
- Kaiser Permanente, Orthostatic hypotension care instructions (roll to side,
  sit up, wait, stand slowly)

## Product signal

Every routine here starts the moment the alarm goes off, and Rep Rot's
alarm only stops after reps. The research adds a safety-flavored detail: the
first minute after waking is groggy and, for some people, lightheaded on
standing. The post tells readers to sit for 30 seconds and stand slowly before
the first squat. That's advice for people, not evidence that any app default
is unsafe (no study or top result reports harm from a few squats on waking),
so it doesn't qualify as a safety finding. The scale-down pattern recurs too:
beginners do squats to the bed edge and incline push-ups on the frame.
