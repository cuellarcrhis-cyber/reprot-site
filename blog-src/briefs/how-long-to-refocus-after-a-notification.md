## Search intent

The searcher wants a number, and to know if it's real, then what to do about
notifications so they lose less focus.

## Must-cover

- The "23 minutes" figure and where it comes from
- Why switching hurts (attention residue / mind wandering)
- Turn off or batch notifications; use Focus / Do Not Disturb
- Self-interruptions, not just pings
- How to get back on task faster

## Questions people ask

- Is it really 23 minutes to refocus? (No: it's time to return, and it varies wildly.)
- How long does it take to get into flow? (15 min is a 1987 book estimate, not a measurement.)
- Does even an ignored notification break focus? (Stothart 2015: yes, in a lab task.)

## Our edge

1. The honest answer: what the 23-minute number actually measured, the
   published 25:26 vs. the interview's 23:15, and the huge spread.
2. A clear "what's known / what isn't" table, including the 15-minute flow
   and brainwave claims.
3. A 30-second "ready-to-resume" note (Leroy & Glomb 2018) as the main move.
4. Current iPhone steps: Scheduled Summary and Focus, with a batching study
   (Fitz 2019) and its caveats, including more FOMO with zero notifications.
