## Our edge

1. A straight answer with a comparison table (what each move is best for, who should start there, the easier version), instead of "it depends".
2. Honest research: one-leg vs two-leg training produced similar strength gains in two small trials (McCurdy 2005, 38 untrained adults; Speirs 2016, 18 rugby players); wall sits topped the systolic ranking in a 270-trial network meta-analysis (Edwards 2023), framed as general information with a doctor note and the acute-rise caveat (Chrysant 2010).
3. A 15-minute legs session at three levels in one table, with scale-downs for every move, including a higher wall sit and a supported split squat.
4. Clear note on what Rep Rot counts (squats) and what it doesn't (lunges, wall sits).

## Sources to cite

- Edwards JJ et al. 2023, Br J Sports Med 57:1317–1326 (270 RCTs, 15,827 participants; isometric −8.24/−4.00 mmHg; dynamic resistance −4.55/−3.04; wall squat top submode for SBP). BMJ release + PDF.
- Chrysant SG 2010, J Clin Hypertens: BP rises during isometric holds, returns to baseline soon after.
- McCurdy K et al. 2005, J Strength Cond Res 19(1):9–15: 38 untrained adults, unilateral = bilateral for early strength/power.
- Speirs DE et al. 2016, J Strength Cond Res 30(2):386–392: 18 academy rugby players, 5 weeks, split squat vs back squat similarly effective.
- Oranchuk DJ et al. 2019, Scand J Med Sci Sports (26 studies): holds at longer muscle lengths built more muscle and transferred better to dynamic performance.
- Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans, 2nd ed.: muscle-strengthening 2+ days/week.
