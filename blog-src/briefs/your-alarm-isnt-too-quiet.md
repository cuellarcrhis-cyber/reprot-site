## Search intent

Someone who keeps losing the fight with their bed wants a few concrete
moves that get them standing tomorrow, plus a sense of whether something is
wrong with them.

## Must-cover

- Alarm out of reach / across the room
- Get light in fast
- Something to get up for; prep the night before
- Enough sleep and a consistent wake time
- When it's more than reluctance (sleep disorders, low mood): see a doctor

## Questions people ask

- Why is it so hard to get out of bed in the morning? (sleep inertia)
- Is hitting snooze bad? (brief answer, Sundelin 2023; full post queued later)
- What if I can't get out of bed most days? (mental health note, 988)

## Our edge

1. A real explanation: sleep inertia hurts decision-making, so the morning
   is the worst time to decide. Decide the night before; make the first move
   physical and automatic.
2. Honest snooze research with caveats instead of "never snooze".
3. A printable 60-second "feet, stand, move, light" routine with an easier
   version for each step.
4. Separates "tired" (a sleep problem) from "unwilling" (a setup problem) and
   handles low mood with care.

## Sources verified

- Sleep Research Society fact sheet, Hilditch & Vincent: symptoms usually fade within about 20 minutes; longer after deep sleep or short sleep.
- Hilditch & McHill (2019), Sleep inertia: current insights, Nature and Science of Sleep: worse after prior sleep loss.
- Sundelin et al. (2023), Journal of Sleep Research: survey n=1,732, 69% snooze at least sometimes, average 22 min; lab n=31 habitual snoozers, 30 min of snoozing cost ~6 min of sleep and didn't hurt morning cognition.
- AASM/SRS consensus (2015): adults 7 or more hours on a regular basis.
- SAMHSA: 988 call/text, 988lifeline.org.
