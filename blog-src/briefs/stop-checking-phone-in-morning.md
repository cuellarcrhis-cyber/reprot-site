## Search intent

They reach for the phone the second they wake and want a concrete way to stop
tomorrow morning: what to change tonight, what to do instead, and whether it
actually matters.

## Must-cover

- Move the alarm off the phone or the phone off the nightstand
- Charge outside the bedroom
- A replacement routine for the first minutes (light, water, movement)
- Build up gradually (delay 5 → 15 → 30+ minutes)
- Why it's worth doing (stress, attention) without overclaiming

## Questions people ask

- Is it bad to check your phone first thing in the morning? (No direct study.
  The cortisol story is weak: Klaas et al. 2025, Proc R Soc B, 201 adults with
  automated sampling, found cortisol rises before waking at the same rate as
  after. Better reasons: what the check displaces, e.g. daylight, and that it
  starts a reactive habit.)
- How long should you wait before checking your phone in the morning? (No
  proven number; 15–30 minutes is a practical target; start with 5.)
- What should I do instead? (Light, water, a few minutes of movement.)

## Facts to use (and their sources)

- Deloitte 2016 US Global Mobile Consumer Survey press release: nearly two-thirds
  of consumers check their phone within 15 minutes of waking, 90% within an hour
  (prnewswire; via search summary).
- Kushlev & Dunn 2015, Computers in Human Behavior 43:220–228: 124 adults,
  randomized crossover, email 3×/day vs unlimited for a week each; lower daily
  stress in the limited week; compliance imperfect (via UBC/summaries).
- Klaas et al. 2025, Proc R Soc B 292(2038), doi 10.1098/rspb.2024.1844: 201
  healthy adults 18–68, automated sampling, rate of cortisol rise no different
  in the hour after waking vs the hour before (via Bristol release/summaries).
- Morning light advances the body clock: Ohashi et al. 2023 (Clocks & Sleep,
  PMC10594521), small lab study: DLMO ~0.24 h later after a day of dim light,
  ~0.18 h earlier after 1 h of bright morning light. Mechanism only; small sample.
- Apple iOS 27 (fetched): Scheduled Summary (Settings → Notifications →
  Scheduled Summary), Lock Screen Count display, Sleep Focus ends at the
  scheduled wake-up time, schedule a custom Focus (Settings → Focus → + →
  Custom → Add Schedule) with its own Home Screen page, Screen Time Schedule
  time windows (iOS 27), Clock alarms that don't turn on Sleep Focus.

## Our edge

1. Honest answer to "is it bad?": debunk the cortisol scare with the 2025
   Bristol study and give the reasons that hold up.
2. Current iOS 27 steps for the first 30 minutes: Scheduled Summary at a later
   time, Count on the Lock Screen, a "Morning" Focus with a one-page Home Screen,
   a morning Screen Time window.
3. A night-before checklist plus a delay ladder (5 → 15 → 30 minutes) instead
   of an all-or-nothing "no phone for an hour".
4. A concrete first-10-minutes routine with a movement scale-down.
