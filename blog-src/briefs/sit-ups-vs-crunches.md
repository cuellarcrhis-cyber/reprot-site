## Search intent

## Must-cover

- Muscles worked (abs vs. abs + hip flexors)

- Easier versions and what to do if your back hurts (plank, dead bug, bird dog)
- "Neither burns belly fat" (we say it without weight-loss framing)

## Questions people ask

- Are sit-ups bad for your back? (Lab and animal data say they load the spine more; the big soldier trials found no difference in injuries or back pain. Pain = stop.)
- Which is better for a beginner? (Crunch: shorter range, easier to control.)
- How many should you do? (Small sets, stop when form goes; 2+ strength days a week.)

## Our edge

1. An honest read of both sides of the back debate: lab work (Axler & McGill 1997; Callaghan & McGill 2001, pig spines) vs. randomized trials in thousands of soldiers (Childs 2009, 2010; George 2011 POLM).
2. A side-by-side table and a "which one for you" decision list, no appearance framing.
3. A ladder from easiest (dead bug / partial crunch) to full sit-up.
4. A simple 2-week starter plan.

## Sources

- Childs JD et al. 2009, Med Sci Sports Exerc 41(11):2072–83, PubMed 19812508 (RCT, 2,616 soldiers 18–35, 12 weeks; sit-up program vs core stabilization: both improved sit-ups, no meaningful difference)
- Childs JD et al. 2010, Phys Ther 90(10):1404–12, PubMed 20651013 (cluster RCT, 1,141 soldiers analyzed; no difference in injury rates; marginal evidence of fewer work-restriction days for low back injuries with core stabilization, 4.2 vs 8.3 days)
- George SZ et al. 2011, BMC Medicine, PubMed 22126534 (POLM cluster RCT, 4,325 soldiers; no difference in back pain needing care between traditional and core stabilization exercise; brief education reduced it)
- Escamilla RF et al. 2006, JOSPT 36(2):45–57, PubMed 16494072 (EMG, 14 people; crunch high abs activity; bent-knee sit-up high rectus femoris, flagged for people with low back problems)
- Axler CT, McGill SM 1997, Med Sci Sports Exerc 29(6):804–11 (9 people, 12 exercises; no single exercise trains all abs with minimal spine load)
- Callaghan JP, McGill SM 2001, Clin Biomech 16(1):28–37, PubMed 11114441 (pig neck spine segments; repeated flexion under modest compression produced herniations)
- Harvard Health, "Want a stronger core? Skip the sit-ups" (planks recruit more balanced muscles; sit-ups work hip flexors)
- Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans, 2nd ed. (muscle strengthening 2+ days/week)
