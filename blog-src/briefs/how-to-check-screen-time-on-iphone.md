## Questions people ask

- What does "pickups" mean on iPhone? (Apple: times you wake the device.)
- Is iPhone screen time accurate? (It's a log, far closer than your guess; it can include other devices when Share Across Devices is on; uncategorized time shows grey.)
- Does Screen Time count notifications? (It counts how many you received; Apple lists it separately.)

## Sources

- Apple, Set up Screen Time for yourself on iPhone (iOS 27): Settings → Screen Time → Daily Average → Week / Day; scroll for Pickups and Notifications; swipe for history; Share Across Devices; widget; Weekly Report notification.
- Apple, same page, iOS 26 version: See All App & Website Activity, Devices, categories, grey = uncategorized.
- Apple, Track app and device usage in Screen Time on Mac (26): Pickups = "the number of times you or a family member wakes a device"; Notifications = "number of notifications received from apps".
- Apple, Change notification settings on iPhone: Settings → Notifications → Scheduled Summary.
- Apple, Wake, unlock and lock iPhone: Raise to Wake is in Settings → Display & Brightness.
- Sewall, Bear, Merranko & Rosen 2020, Mobile Media & Communication, doi 10.1177/2050157920902830 (325 iPhone users, Screen Time as the log; weekly overall iPhone use misestimated by 19.1 h and social media by 12.2 h on average; depression and heavier use predicted bigger errors; cross-sectional).
- Parry et al. 2021, Nature Human Behaviour 5:1535–1547, doi 10.1038/s41562-021-01117-5 (meta-analysis, 106 comparisons; self-reports only moderately correlated with logs and rarely accurate).
- Fitz et al. 2019, Computers in Human Behavior 101:84–94 (randomized field experiment, n = 237 in India, 2 weeks; batching notifications 3x a day: more attentive, better mood, more control; no notifications at all: more anxious. Some co-authors have ties to a related app).

## Product signal

Searchers want to see their own number and whether it's changing. Rep Rot
runs on Screen Time and can't see which apps are blocked or how long they're
used, so the post sends readers to Apple's report to judge whether their
limits are working, and doesn't claim Rep Rot shows usage. The likely recurring
question ("is it working?") is answered by Settings, not the app; that's a
deliberate privacy trade-off, so no change proposed.
