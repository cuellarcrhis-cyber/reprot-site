## Search intent

A step-by-step path from zero to one pull-up they can start this week,
with a realistic sense of how long it takes.

## Must-cover

- Why a pull-up is hard (you lift close to your whole body weight)
- The progression: dead hang, scapular pull, rows, negatives, assisted reps
- A weekly plan with sets/reps and when to move on
- Chin-up vs pull-up (which is easier first)
- Form cues and what not to do (kipping, half reps)
- Equipment: doorway bar, playground, gym; bands optional

## Questions people ask

- Why can't I do a single pull-up? (Strength relative to your size; it's normal.)
- How long does it take? (No honest single number; depends on start; Dayton study shows it can take longer than 3 months.)
- Are chin-ups easier? (Usually; Youdas 2010 found more biceps activation.)

## Our edge

1. Honest evidence: the one controlled study of training non-pullers (Flanagan 2003: 4 of 17 got there in a 3-month program) so readers aren't crushed by "30 days" promises.
2. A ladder with a clear move-on test at every step, no equipment beyond a bar and a chair.
3. An 8-week table with an "easier if needed" column, three short sessions a week.
4. Safety: bar install, shoulder/elbow pain, doctor note, and no kipping for beginners.

## Sources

- Youdas JW et al. 2010, JSCR 24(12):3404–14, PubMed 21068680 (25 adults, 21 men/4 women; surface EMG; chin-up higher biceps and pec activation than pull-up; lats high in all)
- Flanagan SP et al. 2003, Res Q Exerc Sport 74(1):52–9, PubMed 12659476 (college-age women, combined strength + aerobic program; those who succeeded had higher strength relative to size); "4 of 17" from Smithsonian / NYT coverage quoting co-author Paul Vanderburgh
- Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans, 2nd ed. (muscle strengthening 2+ days/week)
