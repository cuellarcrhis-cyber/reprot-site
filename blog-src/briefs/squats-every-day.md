## Our edge

1. A daily plan by level from one test set (chair sit-to-stand up to split squats), not one number for everyone.
2. The research on frequency told honestly: volume matters more than frequency (Grgic 2018 meta-analysis), and small daily doses beat one big weekly dose in a small arm-muscle trial (Yoshida 2022) — with the caveat that it was arms, 36 people.
3. A week-by-week "what happens" built on what's verifiable (soreness peaks 24–72 h, then the repeated-bout effect), no appearance promises.
4. Clear "take a day off" signals and a weekly shape (easy days, one harder day, one off).

## Facts used (verified via search summaries)

- US Physical Activity Guidelines, 2nd ed. (2018): muscle-strengthening on 2+ days/week for all major muscle groups.
- Grgic J et al., Sports Medicine 2018 (PubMed 29470825): frequency linked to strength gains overall, but in volume-equated studies no significant effect (p = 0.421); higher frequency works mainly by adding volume.
- Yoshida R et al., Scand J Med Sci Sports 2022 (PubMed 35908200): 36 young adults, elbow flexors, 4 weeks; 6 eccentric contractions a day, 5 days a week, raised strength >10%; 30 contractions once a week did not significantly change strength.
- Cheung K, Hume P, Maxwell L, Sports Medicine 2003 (PubMed 12617692): DOMS after unaccustomed exercise peaks 24–72 h; repeated-bout effect (Wikipedia/reviews) means the same session hurts less next time.
- Perkin OJ et al., J Aging Res 2019 (PubMed 31687210): 28-day pilot RCT, 20 older adults, home "exercise snacks" including sit-to-stands; large effect sizes for leg power; authors call it preliminary.
