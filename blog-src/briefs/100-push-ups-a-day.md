## Our edge

1. A readiness test that decides which version you do: one honest max set, and a table mapping it to "full challenge / 100 across the day / ramp plan / scaled version". Nobody else ties the plan to a number.
2. A 4-week ramp to 100 with easy days, plus scaled versions (counter, knees) that count as the challenge.
3. Honest evidence: no study tests this challenge; what we do know (Kikuchi 2017, Kotarsky 2018 small trials; Grgic 2018 frequency meta-analysis; DOMS 24–72 h) and US guidelines (2+ days/week, all major muscle groups).
4. Clear stop signs, including the rare but serious one (rhabdomyolysis warning signs after a sudden jump in volume: severe pain, swelling, weakness, dark urine), from Cleveland Clinic and CDC NIOSH.

## Facts used

- No study tests the 100-a-day challenge itself (consistent across all top results; stated as "we couldn't find one").
- Kikuchi & Nakazato 2017, J Exerc Sci Fit (PMC5812864): 18 untrained-to-recreational young men, 2x/week for 8 weeks, 3 sets to failure; push-ups adjusted to 40% of bench 1RM gave similar chest/triceps thickness and 1RM gains to bench press. Small, men only, diet uncontrolled.
- Kotarsky 2018, JSCR (PMID 29466268): 23 men, 3x/week for 4 weeks, progressive push-ups vs bench press, similar 1RM gains.
- Grgic, Schoenfeld & Pedisic 2018, Sports Med (PMID 29470825): frequency matters little for strength when weekly volume is equal.
- Grgic et al. 2022, J Sport Health Sci: no significant strength difference failure vs non-failure.
- Cheung, Hume & Maxwell 2003, Sports Med (PMID 12617692): DOMS peaks 24–72 h after unaccustomed exercise.
- Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans, 2nd ed. (2018): muscle-strengthening 2+ days a week, all major muscle groups.
- Ebben 2011: floor push-up ≈64% of body mass on hands; knees ≈49%; hands on ~61 cm box ≈41% (verified for earlier posts).
- Cleveland Clinic rhabdomyolysis page: symptoms include muscle swelling, weakness, tenderness, dark brown/red/tea-colored urine; jumping into an exercise program too fast is a cause; see a provider right away. CDC NIOSH: symptoms may appear 1–3 days later, pain worse than expected; only a blood test diagnoses it.
