Pattern: almost every result says "progressive overload is the key" and then
gives a flat list of exercises. Few give a way to decide *when* to make a move
harder, and most skip pulling or say "buy a bar". Studies are rarely cited,
and when they are (Scientific Reports 2023) they're not described with sample
size.

## Search intent

They have no gym and no weights and want a simple plan that will actually make
them stronger, plus proof it's not a waste of time.

## Must-cover

- Can bodyweight build strength? (yes, with progression; the honest ceiling)
- Progressive overload explained in plain words: reps, harder variations, slower tempo, pauses, one-leg/one-arm
- The core moves: squat, push-up, lunge/split squat, glute bridge/hinge, plank, a pull/row
- A weekly schedule (2–3 days) with sets and reps
- Beginner scale-downs and safety

## Questions people ask

- Can you build strength with only bodyweight? (Wei 2023, Kotarsky 2018, Calatayud 2015)
- How often should I do bodyweight strength training? (WHO 2020: 2+ days a week, all major muscle groups; ACSM 2009: full-body 2–3 days a week for novices)
- How do I train my back without a pull-up bar? (table/door-frame rows)
- Do I need to go to failure? (Grgic 2022: no)

## Our edge

1. A **ladder system**: five movement patterns, each with 4–5 rungs from
   easiest to hardest, and one clear rule for moving up (3 clean sets of 12,
   then the next rung, aim for 6–8 reps there).
2. A copyable **3-day week** (about 25 minutes) with a beginner version.
3. **Real studies with sample sizes and caveats** (13 women, 23 men, 30
   trained men), plus the honest ceiling: bodyweight gets hard to load for legs.
4. **Pulling solved without a bar** (sturdy table or door frame rows, with a
   safety check), linking to the first pull-up plan for later.

## Sources

- Wei W, Zhu J, et al. "Effects of progressive body-weight versus barbell
  back squat training on strength, hypertrophy and body fat among sedentary
  young women." Sci Rep 2023;13:13505. 13 women (~20 y), 2x/week for 6 weeks;
  both groups gained strength and muscle thickness, no between-group
  difference; body fat fell only in the barbell group. Very small RCT.
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-40319-x
- Kotarsky CJ et al. "Effect of Progressive Calisthenic Push-up Training on
  Muscle Strength and Thickness." J Strength Cond Res 2018;32(3):651–659.
  23 men with some training (push-up 14, bench 9), 3x/week for 4 weeks;
  similar bench press 1RM gains in both. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29466268/
- Calatayud J et al. "Bench press and push-up at comparable levels of muscle
  activity results in similar strength gains." J Strength Cond Res 2015.
  30 trained participants, 5 weeks, elastic-band push-ups vs bench at 6RM;
  similar strength gains. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24983847/
- Grgic J et al. "Effects of resistance training performed to repetition
  failure or non-failure on muscular strength and hypertrophy: a systematic
  review and meta-analysis." J Sport Health Sci 2022. 15 studies, young
  adults; no significant difference in strength or size.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33497853/
- Bull FC et al. "WHO 2020 guidelines on physical activity and sedentary
  behaviour." Br J Sports Med 2020: adults 18–64, muscle-strengthening at
  moderate+ intensity, all major muscle groups, 2+ days a week.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7719906/
- ACSM position stand, "Progression models in resistance training for healthy
  adults," Med Sci Sports Exerc 2009;41(3):687–708: novices, total-body 2–3
  days a week. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/

## Product signal

Every top result's answer to "how do I keep getting stronger without weights"
is to move to harder variations: feet-raised push-ups, split squats,
single-leg bridges. Someone following this plan will want their unlock set to
be the variation they're on now, not the floor push-up. This sharpens the
existing "count beginner variants" item rather than adding a new one: the
same camera test set should include the harder end of the ladder (decline
push-ups, split squats). Verdict: **note** (adds to the 2026-11-05 entry;
