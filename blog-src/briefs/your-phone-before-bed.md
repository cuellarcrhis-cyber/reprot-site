## Search intent

Is using my phone before bed actually bad, why, and what's the one change worth making?

## Must-cover

- Blue light / melatonin (and how strong that evidence is)
- Mental engagement / content
- How long before bed to stop (30–60 min rule of thumb)
- Practical steps: phone out of the bedroom, alarm clock, wind-down
- Night Shift / night mode question

## Questions people ask

- How long before bed should you stop using your phone? (No hard rule; 30–60 min common; the bigger win is not in bed.)
- Does Night Shift help? (BYU 2021 RCT, 167 people aged 18–24: no difference.)
- Is it bad to sleep with your phone next to you? (The habit risk; keep it out of reach.)

## Our edge

1. The contrarian, evidence-backed split: before bed vs in bed (Brosnan 2024 JAMA Pediatrics camera study, Kheirinejad ring study, Exelmans 2016 survey).
2. Honest blue-light read: Chang 2015 was 12 people reading 4 hours on a bright iPad; Night Shift trial found no benefit.
3. Bedtime procrastination named (Kroese 2014) without shaming.
4. A simple "bed is for sleep" setup table with easier versions, and a sleep-trouble safety note.

## Sources

- Brosnan B et al. 2024, JAMA Pediatrics, doi 10.1001/jamapediatrics.2024.2914 (85 recruited / 79 analysed, ages 11–14, body + bedroom cameras, actigraphy; 2 h pre-bed screens not linked to most sleep measures; each extra 10 min in bed ~3 min less sleep, 9 min for interactive use; observational). Otago news release.
- Kheirinejad S, Visuri A, Ferreira D, Hosio S, "Leave your smartphone out of bed", Personal and Ubiquitous Computing, doi 10.1007/s00779-022-01694-w (75 people, app logs + sleep ring, 4 months; ~42% of time in bed on the phone; in-bed use linked to longer sleep latency, more awake time; use outside bed no drop).
- He JW et al. 2020, PLoS ONE 15(2):e0228756 (pilot RCT, 38 college students with poor sleep; no phone 30 min before bed for 4 weeks; shorter latency, longer sleep, better quality, working memory).
- Exelmans L, Van den Bulck J 2016, Social Science & Medicine 148:93–101, PubMed 26688552 (844 Flemish adults 18–94, cross-sectional; 6 in 10 took phone to bedroom; phone use after lights out linked to worse sleep quality, later rise times, fatigue).
- Chang AM et al. 2015, PNAS 112(4):1232–1237, PMC4313797 (12 adults, iPad 4 h before bed × 5 nights vs print; ~10 min longer to fall asleep, less REM, delayed melatonin rhythm, groggier next morning).
- Duraccio KM, Zaugg KK, Blackburn RC, Jensen CD 2021, Sleep Health (BYU; 167 aged 18–24, 7 nights; Night Shift on vs off vs no phone: no overall difference).
- Kroese FM et al. 2014, Frontiers in Psychology, doi 10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00611 (bedtime procrastination; 177 MTurk adults; linked to insufficient sleep).
- Watson et al. 2015 AASM/SRS consensus: adults 7+ hours.

## Product signal

Every top result and the strongest studies point at the same moment: phone
use once you're in bed, not screens in general. Rep Rot's limits are daily
budgets, not time-of-day, so the post can't claim a "bedtime lock" and
doesn't. A time-of-day trigger (apps locked after a set hour until a set)
would fit the core loop, but it's a new trigger type, not a week's change,
and whether it moves retention is unproven. Verdict: **note** (logged one
line, likely to recur with the Downtime and phone-in-bed topics).
