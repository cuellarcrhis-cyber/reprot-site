## Search intent

"Give me a number of rest days for my bodyweight routine, and tell me whether doing a bit every day is OK." They want a number with conditions, then a week they can copy.

## Must-cover

- A number: 1–3 rest days a week for most people, more for beginners
- Why rest matters (recovery, adaptation) without hype
- 48 hours between hard sessions for the same muscle
- Bodyweight isn't automatically "easy": intensity decides
- Active rest: walking, light movement, mobility
- Signs you need more rest; sleep
- Sample weekly schedules

## Our edge

1. Rest days tied to how hard the sets are, with a table by training style (daily easy practice, full-body hard sessions, split), not a single number.
2. "Rest the muscle, not the day": a push/legs alternation for people who want to move daily.
3. The new 2026 ACSM position stand (137 reviews) told honestly: twice a week per muscle group is the target, failure not required, bodyweight counts.
4. Honest evidence: the 48-hour figure comes from small lab studies of hard sets (10 trained men); active recovery helped soreness in a meta-analysis but not perceived fatigue.

## Facts used

- ACSM position stand 2026, Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise 58(4):851–872, umbrella review of 137 systematic reviews (>30,000 participants): resistance training vs none improves strength, size, function; train all major muscle groups at least 2 days a week; training to failure, equipment type and periodization did not consistently change outcomes; bodyweight and home routines produced meaningful gains. Verified via abstract (PMC12965823) and ACSM summaries; full text not read.
- Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2016, Sports Medicine (PubMed 27102172): training a muscle twice a week was linked to more growth than once; too little data on higher frequencies.
- Morán-Navarro et al. 2017, Eur J Appl Physiol (PubMed 28965198): 10 resistance-trained men; sets to failure slowed recovery of jump height and bar speed; non-failure protocols recovered significantly faster between 24 and 48 h. Lifting (squat, bench), not bodyweight.
- Dupuy et al. 2018, Frontiers in Physiology (PMC5932411): meta-analysis of 99 studies; active recovery reduced DOMS (8 groups, 90 participants) but had no significant effect on perceived fatigue.
- Cheung, Hume & Maxwell 2003, Sports Medicine (PubMed 12617692): DOMS peaks 24–72 h.
- Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans, 2nd ed. (2018): muscle-strengthening 2+ days a week.
- CDC MMWR 2016 (Liu et al.): adults 18–60 should sleep at least 7 hours (AASM/SRS recommendation).

Left out: ACSM "48 hours between sessions" wording from the 2011 stand (only seen in secondary summaries), NASM "beginners need 3–4 rest days" (secondary only), overtraining syndrome prevalence, any nutrition/protein advice (brand rules).

## Product signal

Every top result tells readers to take 1–3 rest days a week, and the 2026 ACSM stand says training to failure isn't needed. Rep Rot's limits ask for a set every time they're hit, every day. The post handles it honestly (on rest days pick a light move for muscles you didn't train, or a small set), but the app has no explicit "rest day": a day where the unlock asks for a light move by default. That's a direct conflict between the core loop and the advice every result gives, and it touches safety (sore users forced to rep the same muscle) and retention (users who rest may switch the limit off and not switch it back).
