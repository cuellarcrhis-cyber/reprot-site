## Search intent

A short list of plank versions a beginner can actually do today, in order, with how to do each and when to move on.

## Must-cover

- Knee plank and incline plank as starting points
- Side plank (and the knees-down version)
- Shoulder taps (from knees first)
- Form cues and "stop when the line breaks"
- How long / how often (2–3×/week; short holds)

## Questions people ask

- What's the best plank variation for beginners? (Incline or knee plank; side plank from the knees.)
- Do plank variations work your abs? (Yes, plus shoulders, hips; they train the trunk to resist movement.)
- What if planks hurt my wrists or back? (Forearms, dead bug on the floor; stop and see a doctor/physio for pain.)

## Our edge

1. Organized by what each move trains (front, side, twist), so a reader picks one per family instead of doing 30 random moves.
2. Every move has an easier version and a "move on when" rule.
3. Honest evidence: Schoenfeld 2014 EMG (19 people, one-off 30-second holds; muscle activity, not a training study) shows small changes (longer reach, glute squeeze) make the same plank harder; McGill & Karpowicz 2009 (8 healthy men) is where the side bridge's place as a core staple comes from.
4. A 10-minute, 3-day plan in a table.

## Sources

- Schoenfeld BJ, Contreras B, Tiryaki-Sonmez G, Willardson JM, Fontana F 2014, Sports Biomechanics 13(3):296–306, PubMed 25325773 (19 participants, 4 plank variations, 30 s each, surface EMG; long-lever posterior-tilt plank > traditional for upper rectus abdominis, lower abdominal stabilizers, external oblique; long lever drove most of the difference)
- McGill SM, Karpowicz A 2009, Arch Phys Med Rehabil 90(1):118–126, PubMed 19154838 (curl-up, side bridge, bird dog; EMG + spine motion; 8 healthy young men)
