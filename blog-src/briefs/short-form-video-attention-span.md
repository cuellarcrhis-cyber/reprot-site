## Sources verified (from search summaries)

- Nguyen et al. 2025, Psychological Bulletin 151(9), doi 10.1037/bul0000498 (71 studies, 98,299 participants; cognition r = −.34; attention r ≈ −.38, inhibitory control r ≈ −.41; time spent weakly linked, addiction-style scores more strongly; mostly cross-sectional; no study directly on Reels/Shorts). Via PsyPost, Reason.
- Chiossi et al. 2023, CHI '23 (arXiv 2302.03714): 60 participants, TikTok condition impaired prospective memory; Twitter, YouTube no significant change.
- SDSU 2025 news release: 242 students, 5 minutes of TikTok before reading news, eye-tracking showed more skimming. Lab setting.
- Lorenz-Spreen et al. 2019, Nature Communications 10:1759: collective attention to each topic peaks and fades faster (hashtags 2013–2016, Google Trends, Reddit, Wikipedia).
- BBC 2017 (Maybin), "Busting the attention span myth": the 8-second figure isn't from Microsoft research; no evidence goldfish have short attention spans.
- Castelo et al. 2025, PNAS Nexus, doi 10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf017: 467 adults, two weeks of blocked mobile internet; sustained attention improved, about the same size as 10 years of age-related decline (authors' comparison); adherence imperfect.
- Gloria Mark, University of California News (2024): about 47 seconds average on one screen in 2016–2020 studies.
- Apple Support: Time Allowances / Screen Time; Color Filters (grayscale).
