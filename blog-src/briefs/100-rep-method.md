## Search intent

## Our edge

1. The honest reason it works: muscle grows across a wide load range when sets end close to failure (Schoenfeld 2015 trial, 2017 meta-analysis), but very light work (20% 1RM, Lasevicius 2018) was weaker, and heavy loads still win for max strength.
2. A bodyweight progression table so the rep count stays in a useful range (when 100 gets easy, make the move harder, not the number bigger).
3. A beginner version (50 reps, incline/knee push-ups) and safety stop rules.
4. A clear contrast with grease-the-groove and the 100-a-day challenge, so readers pick the right tool.

## Sources (verified via search summaries)

- Schoenfeld BJ et al. 2015, JSCR 29(10):2954–63. 18 trained men, 8 weeks, 25–35 vs 8–12 reps to failure: similar muscle thickness gains; squat strength +19.6% high load vs +8.8% low load. https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000000958
- Schoenfeld BJ, Grgic J, Ogborn D, Krieger JW 2017, JSCR meta-analysis, 21 studies, sets to failure: hypertrophy similar across loads; 1RM gains greater with heavy loads. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28834797/
- Lasevicius T et al. 2018, Eur J Sport Sci 18(6):772–80. 30 men, 12 weeks, within-subject: 20% 1RM suboptimal; 40–80% all viable. https://doi.org/10.1080/17461391.2018.1450898
- Robinson ZP et al. 2024, Sports Medicine 54(9):2209–31. Meta-regressions (press: 55 studies): hypertrophy increased as sets ended closer to failure; strength similar across a wide range of reps in reserve. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2
- Kikuchi & Nakazato 2017 (push-ups vs bench, 18 men, 8 weeks, similar gains). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29541130/
- Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans, 2nd ed. (muscle strengthening 2+ days/week).
- Men's Health UK (owner's link) and Men's Health Australia Centurion method (rules).
