It's day one. You've seen the videos, you've told one friend, and you're going to do 100 push-ups a day for 30 days.

You get 18 before your arms shake. You rest, you get 11. Then eight, then six. Forty minutes later you're at 100, your shoulders feel like wet sand, and day two is tomorrow.

That's how most 100 push-ups a day challenges go: a great first day, a sore second one, and a quiet stop around day four. Not because the idea is bad. Because nobody checks whether 100 is the right number for them before they start.

Here's how to tell, how to build up to it if you're not there yet, how to run the 30 days without grinding your shoulders down, and what you can honestly expect at the end.

Is 100 push-ups a day too much? Take a two-minute test

It depends on one number: how many good push-ups you can do in a row right now.

  1. Warm up: ten arm circles each way and five easy push-ups against a counter.
  2. Do one set of floor push-ups with your body in a straight line from head to heels and your chest lowering until your elbows are at about 90 degrees.
  3. Stop when your form breaks (hips sag, butt lifts, half reps), not when you collapse. That's your number.

Then find your row. This is our rule of thumb, not a result from a study: keep every set at about half your max or less, so the 100 is practice, not punishment.

What the research says (and doesn't)

We couldn't find a single study of the 100 push-ups a day challenge itself. Every result you'll see after 30 days online is one person's story. What we do have is research on push-ups and on training frequency in general.

  • Push-ups build strength. In a 2017 trial, 18 young men did either bench press or push-ups (adjusted to a similar load) twice a week for 8 weeks. Both groups gained chest and triceps thickness and bench-press strength by similar amounts. A 2018 trial of 23 men found the same for progressive push-ups three times a week for 4 weeks. Both were small, short and men only.
  • Doing them every day isn't magic. A 2018 meta-analysis found that when total weekly work was the same, training a muscle more often didn't add much strength. Daily push-ups help mostly because they add up to a lot of reps, not because "daily" is special.
  • You don't need to go to failure. A 2022 meta-analysis of 15 studies found no significant strength difference between sets taken to failure and sets stopped short. Good news for a challenge built on many small sets.
  • The guideline is lower than 100. US guidelines ask for muscle-strengthening activity at least two days a week, covering all major muscle groups. Push-ups alone cover your chest, shoulders and arms, not your back or legs.

So the challenge can work as a strength and habit builder. It isn't a complete workout, and the number 100 isn't backed by anything except being round.

How to run it:

  • Spread the sets out. Ten sets of ten across a day feels very different from trying to grind out 100 in one session. Any time works: morning, between meetings, before dinner.
  • Stop each set with two or three reps left. If set four looks worse than set one, take a longer break or finish the day on a counter.
  • Keep a tally. A sticky note on the fridge and a pen. Five marks per set. You'll lose count by mid-afternoon otherwise.
  • Repeat a week if you need to. If week 2 left you sore every morning, do it again before moving up. Nothing about this is on a deadline.

Scaled versions count. Hands on a counter, a bench or your bed carry less of your body weight: about 41% with hands raised around 61 cm (2 feet), compared with about 64% on the floor, in one small force-plate study. Knee push-ups came in around 49%. Mix them in whenever floor sets start to look rough.

How to do the 30 days without hurting your shoulders

The risks people mention most are joint strain, no rest and only ever pushing. Each one has a simple fix.

Build in an easy day

Most top challenge guides flag the lack of rest days, and we agree. Do 100 on six days and take one day off, or a light day of 30 easy incline push-ups. If you'd rather keep the streak, that light day keeps it without asking the same muscles for 100 more. More on how many to take in Rest Days for Bodyweight Training.

Add something that pulls

Push-ups work the muscles on the front of your upper body. Balance them with rows: lie under a sturdy table, grab the edge, keep your body straight and pull your chest up to it. Two or three sets of 6–10 on each push-up day is enough. Bend your knees to make it easier. Test the table first; it has to take your weight without tipping.

Vary the push-up

Rotate hand width (a little wider, a little narrower) and the surface (floor, counter, step) through the week. Same total, slightly different stress on your wrists and elbows. If your wrists ache, try fists or push-up handles; Push-Ups and Wrist Pain has seven fixes.

Know the stop signs

Muscle soreness after new exercise usually peaks one to three days later and is normal. These are not:

  • Sharp pain in a wrist, elbow or shoulder, or pain that builds rep by rep.
  • Joint pain that's worse each morning, or still there after a few days off.
  • Muscles that are swollen, very weak or far more painful than the effort deserved, especially with dark, brown or tea-colored urine. That can be rhabdomyolysis, a rare but serious muscle injury linked to ramping up exercise too fast. It can show up days later. See a doctor right away.

If you have a shoulder, elbow or wrist injury, a heart condition or high blood pressure, are pregnant, or haven't exercised in a long time, check with a doctor or physical therapist before you start.

What happens after 30 days of 100 push-ups a day

Here's what you can reasonably expect, based on the research above and how training usually goes. No promises about how you'll look.

  • Week 1: soreness, mostly on days two and three. Sets feel clumsy.
  • Week 2: the same 100 feels easier. Your body gets used to the work, and you get better at the movement.
  • Week 3: the risky week. You feel good, so you rush, skip the easy day or bang out bigger sets. This is where most aches start. Stay on plan.
  • Week 4: retest your max on a fresh morning. Most people who stuck with it can do more in one set than on day one. How many more depends on where you started; nobody can honestly give you a number.

The less obvious result may be the bigger one: 30 days of doing something small, many times a day. That habit is worth more than the 3,000 reps.

And after day 30? Don't just keep doing 100 forever. Swap in harder versions (slower lowering, feet on a step) or drop to three or four push-up days a week and add legs. Slow Down the Lowering is a good next step.

Questions people ask

Can a beginner do 100 push-ups a day?

Yes, if "push-up" means the version that fits you. A beginner doing 100 incline push-ups on a counter across the day is doing the challenge. A beginner forcing 100 floor push-ups with sagging hips is mostly practicing bad form.

Should I do all 100 at once or spread them out?

Spread them out, unless you can already do 40 or more in a row. Small sets with good form add up to the same total with much less strain on your joints, and they fit into a busy day.

Do I have to do them every single day?

No. The research doesn't show daily training beats the same work spread over fewer days. Six days with an easy seventh is a safer version of the same challenge.

TL;DR

  • Test your max set first. Under 25 floor push-ups, ramp up over four weeks or start with incline push-ups.
  • Split the 100 into small sets across the day, each ending with a few reps left.
  • Take one easy day a week, add rows for your back, and vary hand position and surface.
  • No study tests this challenge itself; push-ups do build strength in small trials, but 100 is just a round number.
  • Sharp joint pain, swelling, weakness or dark urine means stop and see a doctor.
